Research

Recent advances in machine learning have unlocked remarkable capabilities for autonomous systems, enabling them to perform increasingly complex tasks in the physical world. As learning-enabled control becomes increasingly prevalent in the physical world, the risks associated with its failures increase correspondingly. My research focuses on the control architectures and statistical foundations of learning-enabled autonomy, with the goal of developing principled approaches for reliable deployment of learning-enabled systems in the physical world.

Layered Control Architectures

As autonomous systems become more complex, their components increasingly operate at different timescales and levels of abstraction, using models of different fidelity. Layered control provides a framework for organizing these components and coordinating decisions across different levels of an autonomous system. My research explores principled approaches to layered control, including the co-design of layers across heterogeneous dynamics, timescales, constraints, and observations, and the use of learning to synthesize lower-level policies while preserving guarantees on their behavior.

Multitask Control Architectures

Data collection from physical systems can be expensive or risky, making it important to exploit structure shared across related systems and tasks. Multitask control provides a framework for learning a common controller across heterogeneous systems and objectives. My research explores system-theoretic approaches to characterizing task similarity and heterogeneity, and to understanding their implications for the performance, stability, generalization, and data efficiency of shared policies.

Statistical Learning for Dynamics and Control

Learning from finite data is fundamental to autonomy, but learned models and predictions are inevitably uncertain. This uncertainty can propagate through the control loop and compromise safety and performance. My research explores statistical foundations for learning dynamics and control, with a focus on quantifying uncertainty in learned models and predictions and translating these estimates into rigorous guarantees for prediction, constraint satisfaction, and safety.
